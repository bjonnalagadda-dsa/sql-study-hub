The databases

Two small databases power every example. Learn their shape once and the queries become easy to read.

MyDatabase (first chapters)

Two tables, customers and orders, linked by customers.id = orders.customer_id. Note order 1004 points to customer 6, who does not exist, and customers 4 and 5 have no orders: perfect for join practice. Customer 2 has a leading space in the name (' John') to practise TRIM.

SalesDB (everything else)

Schema Sales with five tables. Orders is the central fact table; Customers, Products and Employees are the descriptive tables it points to. OrdersArchive has the same columns as Orders and holds older orders.

Relationships: Products 1-to-many Orders (ProductID), Customers 1-to-many Orders (CustomerID), Employees 1-to-many Orders (SalesPersonID = EmployeeID). Employees.ManagerID points back at Employees, which the recursive CTE uses.

Things the data is built to teach

  • Customer 3 Mary has no last name (NULL) and customer 5 Anna has no score: NULL handling.
  • Order 5 has no ship address and order 10 has quantity 0: NULLs and divide by zero.
  • Customer 1 and customer 2 each have several orders: grouping, windows, LEAD/LAG.
  • OrdersArchive repeats OrderIDs: duplicates and ROW_NUMBER.
  • Employees have a manager chain: hierarchy with recursive CTEs.
